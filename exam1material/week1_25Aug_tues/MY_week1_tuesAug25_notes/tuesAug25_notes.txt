Taking a step back...what is logic?

- analysis of an argument according to a set of rules
- given a set of premises (and sometimes a conclusion)


- a VALID conclusion is one that is always true
    anytime all the premises are true


Note that the premises might make no sense!

Logical Arguments with "OR"
    - can be ambiguous! Why? 


I order fries or I order chips.



- in English, how do we interpret "or"?
exclusive (XOR)
without saying, we tend to mean or the other but not both


- in programming? 
an inclusive or, so one, the other, or both would make the statement true


"Or" in THIS class: 
inclusive

When we have an "if/then" statement,
and we know that the "if" part is true?

    ...what can we always conclude?

the then part is also true



/////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

If I don't hear my alarm, then I will be late for class.
I am late for class.

--> What can I conclude? 
nothing
I could have heard my alarm but still been latethe if-then promise is only in one direction

/////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

If I don't hear my alarm, then I will be late for class.
I hear my alarm.

--> What can I conclude? 
nothing
could have been late for other reasons (or not late)




/////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

If I don't hear my alarm, then I will be late for class.
I'm not late for class.

--> What can I conclude? 
I heard my alarm
either heard or didn't hear the alarm
can't be the case that I didn't hear the alarm, because this would mean that i was both late and not late
must be the case that I heard my alarm.


is equivalent to (called the contrapositive):

More if/then logic in programming.


if (num < 4 || x >= 10) {

    y = 17;
}

--> suppose y is 17 here. What do we know?
NOTHING
y could have already been 17, or at least one condition could have been true

/////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

y = 10;
if (num < 4 || x >= 10) {

    y = 17;
}

--> suppose y is 17 here. Can we conclude anything now?
Either num < 4, x >= 10, or both


//////////////////////////////////////////////////////////

if (num < 4 || x >= 10) {

    y = 17;
}

--> suppose y is NOT 17 here. What do we know?
both conditions are false
num >= 4 AND x < 10 (DeMorgan's Law)
